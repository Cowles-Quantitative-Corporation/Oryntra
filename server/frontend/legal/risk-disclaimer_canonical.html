Scans, scores, charts, pattern labels, cross-sectional rankings, watchlists, paper-trading records, Quant Lab reports, Portfolio Lab actions, Minerva outputs, and hypothetical entry, stop, target, trim, hold, buy, sell, or exit language are generalized model-generated research outputs. 2. Historical, simulated, and demo results

Backtests, historical studies, paper portfolios, validation summaries, benchmark comparisons, alpha, win-rate, risk statistics, and demo results can depend on historical, supplied, delayed, incomplete, synthetic, or simulated inputs. They may contain survivorship bias, data errors, model-selection bias, look-ahead errors, corporate-action issues, unrealistic execution assumptions, or costs that differ from live conditions.

Public demo cards may use fixed simulated display data. A TradingView chart shown alongside a demo is a separate visualization and should not be interpreted as the source of the simulated demo result.

3. Model and research risk

Technical indicators are backward-looking transformations of data. Pattern detection uses thresholds and approximations. Cross-sectional rankings depend on the comparison universe and timestamp. Portfolio construction depends on assumptions, constraints, costs, and rebalance rules. A high score, premium feature, model name, or strong historical result does not establish a durable edge or future profitability.

4. Market and execution risk

Securities can lose value rapidly. Gaps, volatility, liquidity, halts, corporate actions, news, spreads, slippage, fees, taxes, leverage, short-sale constraints, and changing market regimes can invalidate a displayed plan. Hypothetical stops, targets, risk limits, correlation overlays, volatility controls, or liquidity controls do not guarantee a fill, limit a loss, diversify risk, or protect capital.

5. Data and technology risk

Provider data may be delayed, corrected, adjusted, incomplete, or unavailable. Embedded charts may use different sources or settings from Oryntra’s calculation path. Internet, browser, backend, database, hosting, payment, provider, advertising, notification, and chart failures may interrupt or alter the service. Cached or previously saved research can become stale.

6. Portfolio Lab and Minerva

Portfolio Lab and Minerva are research tools. Portfolio Lab may record a hypothetical action or allocation; Minerva may compare multiple securities. Neither feature creates a live brokerage position, transmits an order, monitors a brokerage account, guarantees diversification, or establishes that the output is suitable for you.
